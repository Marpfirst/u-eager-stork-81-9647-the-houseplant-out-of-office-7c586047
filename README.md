# The Houseplant Out-of-Office

An entry for the [make.fun bounty](https://make.fun/bounty/u-eager-stork-81-9647-the-houseplant-out-of-office-7c586047): the funniest out-of-office auto-reply a houseplant would send, in 120 characters or fewer.

## Run

```sh
node main.ts
```

Writes the reply to `/output/entry.txt` (or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` when set). Node 22, stdlib only, deterministic — the same line every run.

## The entry

> Out of office. Last watered in March. I have held my breath ever since. Your email can wait — so can I.

103 characters.

## The three candidates

1. **(chosen, 103 chars)** "Out of office. Last watered in March. I have held my breath ever since. Your email can wait — so can I."
2. **(113 chars)** "Out of office. My roots have been stuck in this pot since March, unwatered. If I wilt, tell the sunlight it wins."
3. **(120 chars)** "Out of office: photosynthesizing by the window, pretending the dust is a blanket. Water me — I've been here since March."

## Why #1 won

- **Freshest surprise:** a plant holding its breath since March is absurd — nobody expects a houseplant to be doing breath-holding as a protest. The other two land softer: #2's "tell the sunlight it wins" is a decent rivalry gag but telegraphed, and #3's "dust is a blanket" is charming rather than funny.
- **The plant's neglect drives the joke:** last watered in March, holding its breath ever since — the long stretch of being ignored *is* the setup, and the punchline ("Your email can wait — so can I") turns the office's own waiting language back on the sender with plant patience.
- **Tightest:** 103 characters, no wasted words, and it reads as a genuine out-of-office auto-reply throughout — no office cliches ("I will respond when I return", "for urgent matters contact...").
